TITLE BLOCK
Building exposure (context only - not a cause)
Coastal exposure is context, not a diagnosis. A coastal building can have dampness from rainwater, plumbing, condensation or ground moisture like any other building. Record the exposure, then let the evidence point to the source.

Before you start - safety first

HSE
Water and electricity: do not touch sockets, switches, light fittings or electrical enclosures that are wet, stained or corroded. Do not push moisture meter pins near electrical wiring. Report to a qualified electrician. (CDC Electrical)
  • Only enter roof spaces, sub-floors or cupboards where access is safe, lit and permitted. Never stand between ceiling joists. (Professional practice)
  • View roofs from the ground or a safe window. Do not climb onto roofs - treat all roofs as fragile. (UK HSE)
  • Do not disturb visible mould or strip wallpaper - it can release spores. If you must be close, EPA suggests an N-95 respirator, long gloves and goggles without ventilation holes. (EPA Brief Guide, EPA Remediation Guide)
  • In Pre-Purchase mode, you are a visitor: ask the owner or agent before moving furniture, opening cupboards or lifting covers. (Professional practice)
  • Record what you see. Do not drill, cut or remove finishes. (Professional practice)

    Priority is for organising your investigation. It is not a hazard rating and not a diagnosis. You can change any suggested priority. Priority definitions: professional practice.

    What this tool will not do

    LIMITS
    • It will not tell you a wall has "rising damp", a "leak" or "condensation". A visual inspection alone cannot confirm a moisture source.
    • It does not give "safe" moisture meter percentages. Meter readings depend on the instrument, the material, calibration and method. Record readings as evidence and compare like with like. (Professional practice)
    • It does not replace a building survey, a specialist damp investigation, a plumber, a roofer or an electrician.

    Standards & Measurements

    REFERENCES

    Universal guidance used in this tool

    • Indoor relative humidity: keep below 60%, ideally 30-50%. (EPA Brief Guide, EPA Mold Course)
    • Drying time: if wet or damp materials are dried within 24-48 hours after a leak or spill, in most cases mould will not grow. (EPA Brief Guide, EPA Mold Course)
    • Mould area: less than about 0.9 m² (10 ft²), roughly 0.9 m x 0.9 m (3 ft x 3 ft): in most cases the occupant can clean it. Larger areas or a lot of water damage: consult professionals. (EPA Brief Guide)
    • Contaminated water: damage caused by sewage or other contaminated water needs a professional experienced in contaminated water damage. (EPA Mold Cleanup)
    • HVAC: do not run the HVAC system if you know or suspect it is contaminated with mould. (EPA Mold Cleanup)
    • Do not paint or caulk mouldy surfaces. Clean and dry first. (EPA Brief Guide)
    • Hidden mould may be on the back of drywall, wallpaper or panelling, the top of ceiling tiles, the underside of carpets and pads, in pipe chases, behind furniture and above ceiling tiles. Removing wallpaper can release a large amount of spores. (EPA Brief Guide, EPA Remediation Guide)
    • Wet electrical items: never switch power or use electrical items while standing in water; have a qualified electrician inspect before power is restored. (CDC Electrical)
    • Roofs: treat all roofs as fragile until a competent person confirms otherwise. (UK HSE)
    • Health principle: persistent dampness and microbial growth on interior surfaces and in building structures should be avoided or minimised. WHO states no quantitative health-based threshold can be recommended. (WHO 2009)
    • Dew point: Magnus formula with Alduchov and Eskridge (1996) coefficients (a = 17.625, b = 243.04 °C), valid from -40 °C to 60 °C (-40 °F to 140 °F). Treat results as an estimate. Instrument accuracy matters more than formula accuracy. (Magnus formula)

    Items marked Professional practice reflect general building inspection practice. No single public standard covers them, so no standard is claimed.

      Before you start: how your information is saved

      Your information is saved on this device only.

      Save a backup file regularly.

      Where is my information saved?

      Inside this browser, on this device. Nothing is sent to The Snag Tag or anyone else. That keeps it private, but it also means you are the only one who can keep a copy safe.

      Why could my information seem to disappear?
      • You open the tool in a different browser, a private/incognito window, or on another device.
      • You open a different copy of the tool (for example a downloaded file instead of your link).
      • Your browsing history or site data gets cleared.
      • Safari (iPhone, iPad and Mac) can delete saved information if you don't open the tool for about 7 days.
      • The tool is open in two tabs at the same time. Always use one tab.
      How do I keep my information safe?
      • Press Save a backup now after every session where you change something.
      • Keep the backup file somewhere safe, like your cloud drive or a USB stick.
      • Always open the tool from the same link, in the same browser.
      How do I get my information back, or use another device?

      Open the tool, press Restore a backup and choose your latest backup file. Everything comes back as it was when you saved it.
